Workforce housing in the markets people are moving to.

We do not pursue trophy assets or speculative development. We acquire fully-occupied 1980s–2000s apartment communities in Southeastern metros with employment growth, sustained in-migration and a structural shortage of housing at that price point.

Read the full strategy
Pillar 01

Demand that doesn't depend on optimism

Housing is the last expenditure a household reduces. Workforce apartments in supply-constrained submarkets have historically sustained occupancy through cycles, as the alternative — ownership at prevailing mortgage rates — carries a materially higher monthly cost.

Pillar 02

Value we create, not value we wait for

Returns are generated through a defined business plan: unit renovation on turnover, professional management, control of operating expenses and growth in net operating income. Valuation follows income.

Pillar 03

A conservative debt structure

Fixed or rate-capped agency debt, capitalised reserves, and no short-term financing maturing inside the business plan. The objective is that a difficult two years does not become a permanent loss.

Sources of return.

Returns on a well-acquired apartment community arise from four separate sources.

Quarterly cash flow

Rent collected, expenses paid, debt serviced — what's left is distributed to owners each quarter, targeting a 7–9% annualized preferred return.

Depreciation against your income

Your K-1 passes through your share of depreciation — often accelerated via cost segregation — which can shelter a meaningful portion of the distributions you receive.

Forced appreciation

Apartments are valued on income. Every dollar of net operating income we add through renovation and better operations multiplies into property value at exit.

Loan paydown & inflation hedge

Residents pay down the mortgage on an asset you own, while leases reprice annually — so rising costs flow into rents instead of eroding your position.
